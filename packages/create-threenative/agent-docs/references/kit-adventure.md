# Kit notes: adventure

Kit-specific facts moved out of the kit `AGENTS.md`. Shared conventions: `conventions.md`.

## Overview

Lantern Vale: Wren, a lantern-bearer, takes the Ember Blade from a chest in a walled clearing, then clears the Moss Hollow — a three-room dungeon with a push-block puzzle, a small key, a locked door and the Stone Warden, a boss that is only hurt while dazed. Mosslings hop and bite, Flicker bats circle and swoop, and Thornbacks show a thorned plate that only a cut from behind gets round. Lock-on targeting, a sword slash, a shield, a roll, half-hearts and a heart vessel. Every model is procedural, with no mesh file; five shared tiling photos (`src/render/surface.ts`, licences in `assets/tex-PROVENANCE.md`) give the floor, stone, bark and planks their grain, so a wooden, stone or bark mesh takes `surfaced(color, surface)`, not plain `lit()` (`survives` fails on one: `untexturedSurfaces`); Wren's idle, walk and run are `AnimationClip`s built in code.


## Ownership

ThreeNative owns bootstrap, renderer, fixed-step loop, input, loading, physics bindings, the state bridge and the default look. This repository owns the clearing and the dungeon, Wren's feel, the lock-on, the hostiles, the puzzle, the HUD and every model; `src/game.ts` is portable and React mounts from `src/main.ts`.


## Start every change

`@threenative/physics/navigation` is Recast WASM that Android and iOS refuse, so this kit does not import it: a Mossling steers with a ray to Wren and slides along the wall that ray hits (`src/entities/Mossling.ts`). Keep new hostiles on direct physics queries (`intersectRay`, `intersectShape`, `Area3D`).


## Commands and map

Status: the web target is proven by `pnpm test`, which runs every `playtests/*.playtest.json` in a WebGPU browser. Desktop is claimed only where `native-playtests/survives.playtest.json` ran with `--target desktop`; Android and iOS have not been run for this kit.

`src/entities/Hero.ts` owns Wren — `ADVENTURE_FEEL` is the one table of feel constants, and `playtests/move.playtest.json` proves run speed, stop time and turn time against it. `LockOn.ts` picks and holds a target, `Mossling.ts`, `Flicker.ts`, `Thornback.ts`, `Warden.ts` and `Dummy.ts` are the targets (each hostile telegraphs before it strikes), `Block.ts` and `Switch.ts` are the puzzle, `Door.ts` and `Chest.ts` the passages and the loot; `src/interact.ts` is the one `interact` prompt picker; `src/level/Vale.ts` and `Hollow.ts` build the places and tag the solid meshes; `valeHeight(x, z)` in `Vale.ts` is the vale's one ground (banks, outcrops, a stream bed and a rise to the dungeon, the arena and the play spaces kept level), so place every entity, pickup and `setup.place` through it, and ground steeper than 45° is rock Wren cannot climb; `src/scenes/Play.ts` wires them; `src/render/` is ordinary Three.js and every model in the game (`foliage.ts` is the vale's instanced planting, its floor and the canopy's shade, never solid; `effects.ts` the embers, sparks, fireflies and the companion's trail `Play.ts` fires through four pooled `GPUParticles3D` emitters; `props.ts` the hut, the signs, the pitcher lantern and the companion orb); `src/ui/Hud.tsx` is the one HUD (hearts in halves, keys, the prompt). The camera and the sun are written in `afterPhysics`, never in the scene's frame function, because `moveAndSlide` only queues motion. Keep `playtests/survives.playtest.json` as smoke proof and `touch-controls.ts` aligned with the input map in `src/game.ts`. Collision is the engine's, from your geometry: `buildStaticColliders(ctx, root, { predicate: isSolid })` turns the tagged meshes into fixed trimesh bodies. A `CollisionShape3D` capsule is centred on the body's origin, so the origin is Wren's waist and her rig is pushed down by `halfHeight + radius`; forget that and she floats a body-length above every floor. Entities a scenario places (`player`, `mossling.N`, `flicker.N`, `thornback.N`, `dummy.N`, `warden`, `block`, `chest.sword`, `chest.key`) are registered in `load()`, because `setup.place` runs before `enter()`.

Play gates: `playtests/explore.playtest.json` drives 7200 ticks of seeded random input and fails on any page error; `controls.playtest.json` presses each control the HUD shows, from the spawn, and fails when one changes nothing; `look.playtest.json` fails when mouse motion does not turn `camera.main`; `stride.playtest.json` bounds `footSlip` (`maxSlip` and `maxSlideSpeed: 1`, 1 m/s) on the sole nodes `footL`/`footR`, so the run cycle must follow ground speed: limbs use AnimationPlayer clips with strideRoot, never a timer. A new key binding joins `explore`'s `move` or `actions` and the `controls` scenario in the same change; a mouse button joins `controls` as a `pointerPosition` (`buttons` 1 left, 2 right, 4 middle), because `explore` presses keys only. Controls are the PC scheme: click slash, right-click (hold) shield, Tab or middle click lock on (again: next target, then off), space roll or hop, E open, WASD move, mouse look; J, Shift and Q (hold) stay as secondary keys the HUD omits. The left click is its own input name, `slash`, because the first click only captures the pointer (`look` is `pointerRelative`): `readHeroInput` counts it only when the pointer was already captured on the step before. Mouse look turns the camera by `input.vector("look").x` once captured; the camera never swings back by itself, so there is no auto swing to pause, and while a target is locked its framing owns the view and the mouse is ignored. The slash needs the Ember Blade, so `controls` opens the chest first and the HUD says so until Wren has it.


## Portable authoring contracts

No model here is imported, so there is no authored scale to normalise and the collider is the body: the generated scale-and-snap conventions do not apply. A weapon stays in the hand that holds it: the Ember Blade rides the rig's `RightHand` bone through `attachToBone` in `src/entities/Hero.ts`; parent any new prop the same way.

No material backlighting ships: as in a stock Unreal scene, the sun, the sky light, the torches and each material's own colour are the whole light. The look is the engine's default: `src/render/postprocessing.ts` builds `new WorldEnvironment({ ...qualityPreset(tier), ...overrides })` from `@threenative/core`, which is the stock Unreal 5.8.3 look — the filmic tone curve, metered auto exposure, bloom, ambient occlusion and 4x MSAA. `TN_RENDER_CHAIN` reports what ran. To change it, add an override on that object (`tonemapMode: "aces"`, or `autoExposureEnabled: false` with a fixed `exposure`) in `quality.ts`; do not vendor the chain. `src/render/sky.ts` builds the gradient background and its environment light from `palette.ts`, the height mist on `scene.fogNode` and the sun shafts (soft additive tubes, because the core `godRays` stage does not run on the owned engine yet); `SUN_DIRECTION` aims `lighting.ts`'s sun, and `setIndoors` drops the sky light under the Hollow's roof so the torches carry the rooms.

Two loading conventions come from `@threenative/core`, not from your own loops: `loadAll(items, load)` fetches six at a time and returns results **in the input's order**, and `addInSlices(objects, (object) => ctx.add(object))` attaches in 8 ms slices so hundreds of objects never land in one long frame; override `concurrency`/`sliceSize`, pass `while: () => alive` to stop a torn-down scene without throwing, and `marker: false` silences `TN_LOAD_ALL`/`TN_ADD_SLICES` but never the measurement.


## Budget real time for the look

It is a scene-shape verdict, so answer it by moving the draw and object counts, not the engine. `npx threenative doctor` repeats the last verdict and the `DEV_MODE=true` chip shows it on screen; `TN_FRAME_SPANS=1` adds the render phase's own span tree when you need to know which part costs the milliseconds. Do not touch a transform you do not need to: three skips the per-object binding update when nothing changed. `markStatic(root)` composes a never-moving subtree once, and `invalidateStatic(object)` announces a write inside one.
