# PRD-573 engine benchmark results, 2026-10-12

These are the results of the native C++ engine (`tn-engine-desktop`, `tn-engine-web`) against the legacy three.js arms (`tn-desktop`, `tn-web`) on the engine-load-test R ladder at 4,096 objects. The PRD is [PRD-573](../../PRDs/performance/PRD-573-unreal-class-performance-in-release-cycles.md). The raw reports are untracked under `artifacts/engine-load-test/prd-573/`, and the live dashboard is `pnpm exec tsx scripts/perf-live.ts`.

## Native desktop, real GPU

Setup: RTX 2080 with a per-process NVIDIA ICD, on the real KDE display `:0` (Xwayland). Both arms ran on the same display, and a temporary KWin rule gave each window the exact requested size. The command was `pnpm bench:engines --pair tn-desktop,tn-engine-desktop --rounds 3`, waiving drawCalls only. Triangles were equal on both arms. Host load was 8 to 21, from other sessions, so read the ratios and not the absolute ms.

Notes:
- **Present.** On a real display, present costs 0.10 to 0.16 ms on both arms. Under Xvfb it costs about 75 ms on both arms, which is why the KPI excludes it on that lane.
- **Hitches.** The engine's hitch count (5 to 12, against 0 to 1) is relative to each arm's own median. The engine median is much lower, so this is a watch item. Absolute p95 and p99 are lower on the engine at every rung.
- **What remains is irreducible on the engine side.** The NVIDIA driver plus `clock_gettime` take 43% of self time. fdlibm sin/cos parity math takes 3%. JS work that legacy also does takes 5%. Cold-object loads in `ProjectedCull::apply` take 3%, and prefetch failed its A/B.
- **Invalid runs, not cited.** In `gpu-R5-desktop` the engine arm ran under Xvfb (fixed in 14d887e24). In `gpu-R5-desktop2` to `5`, the window manager shrank the window and the surface guard refused the run.

## Web, browser WebGPU

Timings are page main-thread wall time from `performance.now()`, not a CPU clock. The arms are tn-web against tn-engine-web at R5@4096, 1920x1080, DPR 1, with headed Chrome and `--browser-recipe webgpu`.

| Run | Build | Host | tn-web p50 | engine-web p50 | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `web-baseline` | lane web host | laptop, Intel gen-12lp | 4.33 | 29.00 | 0.15x |
| `web-final-2` | cb4ecdb82 (bridge cuts 1-4) | laptop, Intel gen-12lp | 6.51 | 7.06 | 0.92x |
| `web-desk-confirm-1` | 6760f0771 (collect cut) | desktop RTX 2080, Xvfb, load ~14 | 10.61 | 10.47 | 1.01x |

Compare ratios within one run, not absolute ms across hosts.

Notes:
- **Bridge cuts** cut JS-to-Wasm crossings from 38,308 to 52 per frame.
- **WebGPU calls are not the bottleneck.** The engine makes 117 WebGPU calls per frame against tn-web's 554. The Emscripten glue plus uploads are about 1% of samples.
- **The collect cut** stopped scanning all held wrappers each frame. The A/B gave p50 14.79 to 11.08 ms (every B below every A), and render 8.28 to 4.23 ms.
- **The open gap** is the game phase: 5.05 ms on the engine against 1.75 ms on tn-web.
