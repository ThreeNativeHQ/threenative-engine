# PRD-573 engine benchmark results, 2026-10-12

These are the results of the native C++ engine (`tn-engine-desktop`, `tn-engine-web`) against the legacy three.js arms (`tn-desktop`, `tn-web`) on the engine-load-test R ladder at 4,096 objects. The PRD is [PRD-573](../../PRDs/performance/PRD-573-unreal-class-performance-in-release-cycles.md). The raw reports are untracked under `artifacts/engine-load-test/prd-573/`, and the live dashboard is `pnpm exec tsx scripts/perf-live.ts`.

## Summary

| Lane | R5 ratio | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native desktop, real GPU, frame p50 | **2.80x** | v1 KPI (2x) met |
| Native desktop, Xvfb, thread CPU without present | 2.10x | Agrees with the real-GPU lane |

A ratio is legacy over engine. Above 1 means the engine is cheaper.

Notes:
- **Present.** On a real display, present costs 0.10 to 0.16 ms on both arms. Under Xvfb it costs about 75 ms on both arms, which is why the KPI excludes it on that lane.
- **Hitches.** The engine's hitch count (5 to 12, against 0 to 1) is relative to each arm's own median. The engine median is much lower, so this is a watch item. Absolute p95 and p99 are lower on the engine at every rung.
- **What remains is irreducible on the engine side.** The NVIDIA driver plus `clock_gettime` take 43% of self time. fdlibm sin/cos parity math takes 3%. JS work that legacy also does takes 5%. Cold-object loads in `ProjectedCull::apply` take 3%, and prefetch failed its A/B.
- **Invalid runs, not cited.** In `gpu-R5-desktop` the engine arm ran under Xvfb (fixed in 14d887e24). In `gpu-R5-desktop2` to `5`, the window manager shrank the window and the surface guard refused the run.

## Web, browser WebGPU

Timings are page main-thread wall time from `performance.now()`, not a CPU clock. The arms are tn-web against tn-engine-web at R5@4096, 1920x1080, DPR 1, with headed Chrome and `--browser-recipe webgpu`.

| Run | Build | Host | tn-web p50 | engine-web p50 | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `web-baseline` | lane web host | laptop, Intel gen-12lp | 4.33 | 29.00 | 0.15x |
| `web-final-2` | cb4ecdb82 (bridge cuts 1-4) | laptop, Intel gen-12lp | 6.51 | 7.06 | 0.92x |
| `web-desk-confirm-1` | 6760f0771 (collect cut) | desktop RTX 2080, Xvfb, load ~14 | 10.61 | 10.47 | 1.01x |
| `web-desk-confirm-2` | 1b1926f55 (own-property members, allocation-free rotation) | desktop RTX 2080, Xvfb, load ~11 | 7.96 | 5.63 | **1.41x** |

Compare ratios within one run, not absolute ms across hosts.

Notes:
- **Bridge cuts** cut JS-to-Wasm crossings from 38,308 to 52 per frame.
- **WebGPU calls are not the bottleneck.** The engine makes 117 WebGPU calls per frame against tn-web's 554. The Emscripten glue plus uploads are about 1% of samples.
- **The collect cut** stopped scanning all held wrappers each frame. The A/B gave p50 14.79 to 11.08 ms (every B below every A), and render 8.28 to 4.23 ms.
- **The open gap** is the game phase: 5.05 ms on the engine against 1.75 ms on tn-web.

## Real-game benchmark: Midway on web (2026-10-13)

The same Midway build (`midway-open-pacific` 6498dc2) ran on three.js (`engine: "legacy"`) and on the C++ Wasm engine (`engine: "native"`). Harness: `scripts/midway-bench/`.

**Parity first.** Three engine gaps made the native cockpit wrong while the flight still passed. Each fix has a test that failed before it:
1. `Material` and `BufferGeometry` had no `uuid`, so the game's cockpit batcher merged every mesh into one material.
2. Loaded image textures had no mipmaps.
3. The Wasm renderer ignored `antialias` and drew 1 sample where three.js drew 4.

After the fixes, a fresh visual judge rated takeoff, cockpit and after NEUTRAL against legacy. The parity gate (`scripts/midway-bench/parity.ts`) now blocks any timing run whose frames fail.

**Timing** (airborne start through the game's `#start-air` button, cruise with no combat, 5+5 alternating, host load 8 to 17, medians):

| arm | p50 ms | p95 | p99 | hitches | startup | fps | CPU ms/frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C++ Wasm engine | 20.3 | 34.9 | 48.6 | 15 | 38 s | 48 | 23.9 |
| three.js | 14.5 | 34.3 | 45.9 | 69 | 17 s | 58 | 17.9 |

**Second pair** (the repo harness `scripts/midway-bench/flight.sh`, parity PASS, load 8 to 19, 3 to 8 runs per arm), native over legacy:

| view | p50 ms | p99 | hitches | CPU ms/frame | startup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| air cruise | 21.0 / 14.5 | 49.3 / 45.9 | 10 / 69 | 24.2 / 17.9 | 40 / 17 s |
| cockpit view | 22.7 / 21.3 | 41.1 / 45.7 | 2 / 7 | **26.9 / 31.2** | 46 / 26 s |

In cockpit view the engine uses less CPU per frame than three.js and p50 is close. In air cruise it is slower. Memory: the engine page holds about 1.9 GiB of Wasm memory plus a 410 MiB JS heap; three.js holds about 620 MiB of JS heap (one reading).

**Result: on the real game, the web engine is slower (about 1.4x on p50, 1.3x on CPU per frame, 2.2x on startup).** It hitches less. The 1.44x win on the R5 bench does not carry over to Midway yet. The spread is wide: native p50 ran 14.0 to 23.2 ms and legacy 8.5 to 16.3 ms. Memory was not collected. The web lane now profiles Midway's CPU per frame first, then startup.
